Why annual vaccinations still matter for your pet

PodVet Editorial · September 2026

SM

Dr. Sana Malik, DVM

Lead Veterinarian & PodVet Medical Editor · Sep 12, 2026

Vaccines are one of the cheapest, most effective ways to protect a pet from disease — yet they are also one of the most misunderstood. Some owners skip boosters because "my pet is indoors", or because they worry about over-vaccination. So what does the evidence actually say?

Why an old vaccine stops working

Immunity doesn't last forever. After the initial puppy or kitten series, antibody levels fade over time. For core diseases like canine distemper and parvovirus — which are often fatal — a booster keeps the pet protected as the immune memory declines.

Annual means "at least every year" for many boosters. Some vaccines now carry three-year labels, but your vet chooses the schedule based on your pet's age, local disease risk and lifestyle. Skipping a year while a dog still has immunity rarely causes harm; skipping several leaves a gap diseases fill quickly.

Indoor pets are still at risk

Indoor cats and small indoor dogs still go to groomers, boarding kennels, parks and the vet — all places where viruses are shed. Parvovirus can survive in soil for months and can be carried on shoes. "Indoors" dramatically reduces risk; it does not remove it.

What about over-vaccination?

Modern vaccines are remarkably safe, and serious reactions are rare. Vets match booster intervals to risk — a low-risk adult cat may get core vaccines less often than a therapy dog that meets dozens of dogs weekly. The takeaway

Vaccination is a conversation between you and your vet. Keep your records up to date, keep boosters on schedule, and when in doubt — ask. In a clinic, that's exactly what PodVet is built for: clear records, timely reminders, and happy, healthy patients.
